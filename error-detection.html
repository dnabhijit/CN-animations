Split the data into fixed-size words, add them together (wrapping any carry-out back around — "end-around carry"), then send the complement of that sum as the checksum. The receiver adds everything, including the checksum: all 1s means no error detected.

Press Next step to begin computing the checksum.

CRC treats the message as a polynomial and divides it by a fixed generator using modulo-2 (XOR) division. The remainder becomes the CRC bits. Step through the division one row at a time, exactly as you would by hand.

Data = 1101011011, Generator = 10011 (degree r = 4). Press Next step to append zeros and begin dividing.

Redundant bits sit at power-of-2 positions (1, 2, 4, ...), each checking a specific subset of the other bits. After transmission, recomputing them produces a syndrome that points directly at any single flipped bit — enough information to correct it, not just flag it.
